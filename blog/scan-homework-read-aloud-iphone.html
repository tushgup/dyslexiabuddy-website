How to Scan Homework and Hear It Read Aloud on iPhone

November 25, 2026 · 7 min read

It is 7 p.m., the worksheet is due tomorrow, and it is two pages of small print. For a child with dyslexia, just getting through the words can use up all their energy before they reach the actual questions.

An iPhone can turn that paper page into text and read it aloud. Here is how to do it with DyslexiaBuddy, how to do it for free with the tools already on your iPhone, and what to try when a scan does not come out right.

Why scanning homework helps

Text-to-speech usually needs digital text, but a lot of homework still comes home on paper. Scanning fixes that. You can use OCR, the technology that reads printed words from a picture, with worksheets and paper documents by scanning or photographing them (Understood).

Many text-to-speech tools highlight each word as it is read, so your child can see the text and hear it at the same time. That makes reading multisensory (Understood). It is also a recognized support: letting a student use text-to-speech is one of the classroom accommodations Understood lists for dyslexia (Understood).

How to do it with DyslexiaBuddy

Our app works on iPhone and iPad. Scanning a page and hearing it read aloud with the standard voice are free.

1. Tap Scan

Open the app. On the Home screen, tap Scan (it says "Scan printed pages"). Lay the worksheet flat on a table in good light.

2. Photograph each page

Hold your iPhone above the page and follow the camera's prompts to capture it. For a two-page worksheet, capture both pages, then tap Save. The app turns the pictures into text and opens it in the reader.

Tip: if you only need a line or two, such as one question, tap Camera on the Home screen instead. It reads the text in front of the camera without saving a whole page.

3. Press play and follow along

Press play. Each word lights up as it is spoken, so your child can keep their place. You can slow the voice down or speed it up under Speed in the read-aloud settings. Many kids like a slower pace for questions and a faster one for text they have already heard.

4. Make the page easier to look at

Open Text settings to change the font, size, and spacing. Our guide to the best font for dyslexia explains which choices tend to help.

5. Check a tricky word

Press and hold any word to open its Word Card. It shows the meaning, breaks the word into syllables, and plays the pronunciation. This helps with subject words your child has never seen before.

If you want a more natural voice, the AI voices are part of Premium: $3.99 a month, $39.99 a year, or $69.99 once, with a 7-day free trial. The standard voice works fine for homework.

How to do it for free with iPhone tools

You can get the same result with the tools built into your iPhone. It takes a few more taps.

  1. Turn on Speak Selection. Go to Settings > Accessibility > Read & Speak > Speak Selection (Apple Support). On the same screen, you can choose how the words are highlighted as they are spoken.
  2. Get the text with Live Text. Point the Camera at the worksheet. When a yellow frame appears around the text, tap the Live Text button (it looks like lines of text inside a frame), then touch and hold to select the words and copy them (Apple Support). You can also do this from a photo you already took (Apple Support).
  3. Paste and listen. Paste the text into Notes, select it, and tap Speak (Apple Support).

If the text is already on screen, for example a worksheet the teacher shared as a PDF, Speak Screen is quicker. Turn it on in Settings > Accessibility > Read & Speak > Speak Screen, then swipe down with two fingers from the top of the screen to hear everything on it (Apple Support).

The free route works well for a page now and then. A reading app is more useful when scanning is part of every evening: the page is saved, word highlighting is already set up, and the reading settings stay the way your child likes them. Writing the answers

Hearing the questions is half the job. If writing is hard too, Dictation lets your child say an answer instead of typing it. Tap the Dictate button on the keyboard, speak, and tap it again to stop (Apple Support). If you do not see it, turn on Enable Dictation in Settings > General > Keyboard. Using speech-to-text for writing is another accommodation Understood lists for dyslexia (Understood). If your child's teacher allows typed answers, this can take a lot of pressure off.

Keep sessions short. Short breaks between subjects can make homework time more productive (Understood).

If the scan does not come out right

  • Words are missing or wrong. Scan again with more light, no shadow from your hand or phone, and the phone straight above the page.
  • The order is jumbled. Worksheets with columns or boxes can come out in a strange order. Scan one part at a time, or on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, try Clean Up Text in the reader's menu, which looks for broken lines, stray hyphens, and mixed columns.
  • There is a chart or a math problem. Pictures and equations do not read aloud well. Pause and look at that part of the page together.
  • The voice is too fast. Lower the speed. There is no prize for finishing first.

Questions parents ask

Can an iPhone read a worksheet aloud for free?

Yes. Use Live Text to copy the words from a photo of the page, paste them into Notes, then select them and tap Speak. It takes a few more taps than a reading app, but it costs nothing.

Is scanning free in DyslexiaBuddy?

Yes. Scanning pages and hearing them read aloud with the standard voice are free. The natural AI voices are part of Premium, which costs $3.99 a month, $39.99 a year, or $69.99 once, with a 7-day free trial.

Is listening to homework cheating?

No. Text-to-speech is a recommended classroom accommodation for dyslexia. It lets your child focus on the content of the work rather than decoding every word.

Will text-to-speech stop my child learning to read?

No. Understood notes that text-to-speech won't slow down the development of reading skills. But reading tools on their own will probably not improve reading skills, so keep up reading practice and teaching too.
