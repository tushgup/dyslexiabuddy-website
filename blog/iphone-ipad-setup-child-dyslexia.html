How to Set Up an iPhone or iPad for a Child with Dyslexia

October 18, 2026 6 min read

Kids with dyslexia are not lazy. Reading just takes more out of them. The good news: the iPhone or iPad already in your home has free settings that take some of that work away. This guide shows you where they are, in about 15 minutes.

Each step below is free and built into iOS and iPadOS. At the end, we show how a reading app made for dyslexia can do even more for homework and reading time.

Before you start: sit down with your child and try each setting together. Every reader is different. A setting that helps one child can bother another, so let them choose what stays on.

1. Make the text easier to see

Small, thin text is harder to read for everyone, and much harder for a tired reader. Go to Settings › Accessibility › Display & Text Size.

  • Larger Text: drag the slider until your child can read a message without leaning in. Many apps follow this size.
  • Bold Text: makes letters thicker and clearer across the whole device.

2. Let the device read aloud

Hearing the words while seeing them is one of the biggest helps for kids with dyslexia. Go to Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak. (On older versions of iOS, this is called Spoken Content.)

  • Speak Selection: your child selects any text and taps Speak.
  • Speak Screen: swipe down from the top of the screen with two fingers, and the device reads the whole page.
  • Highlight Content: lights up each word as it is read, so your child can follow along with their eyes.
  • Voices: try a few. Apple offers free Enhanced and Premium voices that sound much more natural. Pick the one your child likes.

3. Turn on Accessibility Reader

Accessibility Reader (iOS 26 and later) opens the text of almost any app in a clean, calm view. You choose the font, colors, and spacing, and it can read the text aloud.

  1. Go to Settings › Accessibility › Read & Speak › Accessibility Reader and turn it on.
  2. Open an app with text, such as Safari, and triple-click the side button (or the Home button) to open the reader.
  3. Tap the … button, then Customize Reader. Try a warm background color, a bigger size, and more line and word spacing.
  4. Tap Play to hear the text, with each word highlighted.

4. Make writing easier with dictation

Spelling can slow down great ideas. Tap the microphone on the keyboard and let your child say their answer. Then read it back together and fix any words. This keeps homework about thinking, not only spelling.

5. Help with focus

  • Background Sounds (Settings › Accessibility › Audio & Visual) plays calm noise, such as rain or ocean, to cover distracting sounds.
  • A Focus (Settings › Focus) can silence notifications during homework time.
